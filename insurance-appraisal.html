Stated-Value & Agreed-Value Policy Appraisals

Standard auto insurance policies pay actual cash value in the event of a total loss — which may be far less than what you paid or what your vehicle is truly worth, especially for classic cars, modified vehicles, and limited-production automobiles. A stated-value or agreed-value policy locks in a predetermined insurance value, so you know exactly what you'll receive if the worst happens.

Collector car insurers such as Hagerty, Grundy, and American Collectors Insurance require a certified appraisal to issue or renew agreed-value policies. The appraisal must be performed by a credentialed, independent appraiser — not estimated from a guidebook. Salt Lake City Auto Appraisers' ASCAA certification and USPAP-compliant appraisal methodology meet the requirements of all major collector car insurance companies operating in Utah.

We also provide regular reappraisals for vehicles that have appreciated in value, ensuring your coverage keeps pace with the market. Many classic cars in the Salt Lake City metro area have increased significantly in value in recent years — don't let outdated policy limits leave you underinsured.

Insurance Dispute Appraisals & the Appraisal Clause

When you and your insurance company cannot agree on the value of your vehicle following a claim, most Utah auto insurance policies contain an appraisal clause giving you the right to invoke an independent appraisal process. Understanding and using this clause effectively can make a significant difference in your settlement.

Under the appraisal clause, both you and the insurer each select a certified, independent appraiser. If the two appraisers cannot agree on value, they jointly select an umpire whose decision is binding. Salt Lake City Auto Appraisers has extensive experience in appraisal clause disputes and has helped the Wasatch Front vehicle owners recover thousands of dollars beyond initial insurer offers.

Utah Statute § 627.7015 also provides mediation options for first-party insurance disputes, and Utah Statute § 626.9641 prohibits unfair claim settlement practices. We work in conjunction with your attorney or directly with the insurer to document, negotiate, and if necessary arbitrate your claim to a fair resolution.
